# Source checks: durable stores and their invariants

One row per file the link (`link/core.ts`, `link/landings.ts`, `link/main.ts`, `link/publish.ts`) or the record keeper
(`record/main.ts`) writes under /data that a tap, an owner line or a card's buttons rest on, then the rest. Columns: who
writes it (and in which function), what is durable before what, what rebuilds it after a restore of the App's state,
what it decides, and the clock behind each time it holds.

Clock rules: whether a tap or an owner line counts is decided only by Discord snowflakes (a message or interaction id),
never by the Pi wall clock: nothing he sends or taps is judged by the Pi's clock. A Pi wall clock use decides only when
something is posted, when a card's buttons come off (through the offset the link measured against Discord's stamps), how
far back to read, or how long something is kept. A bound inside one process runs on the monotonic clock. "fsynced" means
the file (and its folder when the file is new or renamed) is fsynced before the call returns (`applog.ts` appendLog, the
link's `writeDurable`, the link state's store in `publish.ts`, the landing ledger's `writeDurable`, the keeper's
`writeAtomic`). A record in an append-only log is one write under 4000 bytes (`MAX_RECORD_BYTES`), opened for append, so
two writers' records never interleave; a longer one is refused before anything is written. Round 20: free-text fields (a
settle reason from GitHub, a notice's reason, an error: `FREE_TEXT_FIELDS`, in nested objects and arrays too) are cut
against a byte budget for the whole serialised UTF-8 record (`fitRecord`): each to 600 bytes on a code-point boundary,
then the longest halved until the framed record is under 4000 bytes; nesting past 8 levels is replaced by a marker; a
record whose other fields alone are over the limit is refused with a bounded message, nothing written. A short write is
truncated back to the file's length before it, then refused; every reader skips a torn final line and reports it (one
line per file and length); an append first cuts off a torn final line, so a new record never lands glued to one; and
every append to a log is serialised by an OS advisory lock (flock) on a file beside it (`withLogLock`, `<log>.flock`),
held through the torn-tail repair, the append and the fsync, and released by the kernel when its holder dies, so a dead
holder is recovered by whichever waiter gets the lock next: no takeover, and no two waiters ever hold it at once. Round 21:
flock is resolved from libc through a soname fallback list (`LIBC_NAMES`: libc.so.6, libc.so, libc) and probed at start by
the link and by the keeper (`probeLock`, taking and releasing it on a probe file in the real log folder): when it can't be
loaded or taken, each refuses to start with a status line he sees (`lockRefusal`: the link posts it, the keeper's goes
through Cain), and tries again hourly; a would-block for `LOCK_WAIT_MS` (5 seconds, monotonic) is a `LockTimeout`, nothing
written, and every append caller holds what it was doing and retries (`LOCK_CALLERS` in `applog.ts`: the consumed-key log's
intent, consumed record, dropped intent and landed or unlanded marks wait for the next round, the request staying queued
or the intent resolved from the history; the held log's held record answers busy with the buttons kept, and its
releasing, released and replay marks are retried next sweep; the landing ledger's settle log answers false with nothing
settled or used, so the same command runs again), never a dropped record or a crashed loop; any other flock error is
reported at once with its errno and name (`LockError`), never as another writer holding it; an arm64 run of these tests is
a CI job (`arm64-flock`). Round 22: the start probe also requires errno to resolve (`ERRNO_SYMS`; without it every
contention would be told at once as a lock error) and runs a contention self-test (a child process, `applog.ts --hold`,
holds the probe file's lock and the probe's own non-blocking try must answer would-block); either failing refuses the
start with its precise reason. The refusal, its line and the hourly retry are `startgate.ts` (`lockGate`), which both
entry files call and the tests drive. The `arm64-flock` CI job is evidence for symbol resolution and the foreign-function
path on arm64 (an arm64 container under user-mode emulation, QEMU, on an x86 host), not proof of the Pi's kernel: the Pi
start probe plus the combined fault scenario are the real arm64 gate. Making the job a required check is a ruleset change
only the owner can make. `LOCK_CALLERS` is complete: every call site of `appendLog`, `appendLogHeld`, `appendInstall` and
`withLogLock`, and every repairing read, in the link and the keeper matches one entry's source fragments (`LOCK_SITES`), a
test checks both directions, and each entry says what its caller does on a `LockTimeout` (the issued-key log's key record:
no key, so the card is never made tappable; its run, void, armed and supersede marks: not written, retried by the sweep or
the next start; the expired-key index and the strip queue: retried next sweep, the card edit idempotent; the keeper's
mirror and the install log: retried next round; the push guard: nothing pushed this round). The keeper's per-round torn
check of the consumed-key log takes its flock with one non-blocking try (`readLog` repair `"try"`): on would-block the
repair is skipped to the next round, so a round never stalls behind a writer there. A reader never truncates by default (`readLog`'s repair is off): `repair: true` truncates a torn
tail only under the log's flock, re-read inside it, and only the keeper's single-writer mirror (`record-issued.log`) may
repair without the lock (`repair: "mirror"`; any other file throws). Round 23: the consumed-key log's writers (intent,
consumed record, dropped intent, landed mark, drop, push guard) wait `consumedWait()` for its flock (`CONSUMED_WAIT_FACTOR`
times `LOCK_WAIT_MS`, then 30 seconds), well above the guarded push's hard timeout (`PUSH_TIMEOUT_MS`, then 20 seconds; round 24 raises both, below), so a push
held to its timeout never makes another of them time out; the lock is released by `withLogLock`'s finally whatever the push
did. The `LOCK_SITES` check scans the whole source tree (every `.ts` file under `cain/rootfs/opt/cain`), the log module's own
primitives included, and fails on any unlisted lock-acquiring call or repairing read; the intent-log callers each have a
held-past-timeout test. The start probe's contention helper is started with the running executable, its own runtime flags
(`process.execArgv`) and the log module's file (`probeHelperArgv`), so it runs wherever the entry point does; a helper that
can't be spawned or exits before it takes the lock is its own refusal ("failed to start"), told apart from one that runs but
can't take the lock and from a flock that doesn't contend, and a test launches the real keeper entry file as the image does.
Round 24: the consumed-key log's writers wait 90 seconds (`CONSUMED_WAIT_FACTOR` 18 times `LOCK_WAIT_MS`), above the push's
wall-clock ceiling (`PUSH_TIMEOUT_MS`, 75 seconds, the run script's `RECORD_PUSH_TIMEOUT_S`, clamped below that wait by
`netTimeout`); git's low-speed limit (`NET_LOW_SPEED_BYTES` a second for `NET_LOW_SPEED_S`) is the primary abort. Every network
call of the keeper (the opening fetch, a clone, the push, the post-release read-back, the drop's fetch, the Date header's
request) runs through `netRun`: its own session (setsid), a hard wall-clock limit, the whole session killed on expiry, stdin
closed. The keeper's start probe (`keeperLockProbe`) refuses to start without the session tool, with `NET_SESSION_WHY`. The
whole-tree scan is backed by a lint (`lockLint`, a source test): no re-export, aliased or namespace import, value use, bracket
access or generic-form call of a lock primitive, and no generic-form repairing read; a test launches the link's real entry
file too, as the image does.
Round 25: the guarded push runs under one deadline (`lockedBound`: `consumedWait()` less two lock waits, 80 seconds) for
its whole section: the guard's checks, the push (given what is left less one lock wait for the scan, never more than the
network ceiling; less than `PUSH_TIMEOUT_MIN_MS` left refuses with kind `deadline`), the scan of what it carried, and the
read-back after the release; the time held under the lock is logged and returned (`heldMs`) for the resilience run. The
network wrapper (`netRun`) has no fallback: without the session tool it runs nothing and answers `refused` with
`NET_SESSION_WHY` (git then throws), and the image build fails without the tool (the Dockerfile's check). The textual lint is
replaced by a TypeScript-compiler syntax-tree check (`lockLintAst`, a source test): every reference to a lock primitive or to
`readLog` is the callee of a direct, non-optional call (no call, apply or bind, no destructuring alias, no optional call,
no property or element access, no value use; a comment can't hide a call), declared only in its own module, and every
lock-taking call (a locker, or a `readLog` whose repair isn't false or "mirror") is matched by a `LOCK_SITES` fragment. The
link's entry file is launched in a test as a non-root user (nobody, when the tests run as root: the arm64 job's container
too), so a path the test forgot to move fails instead of creating /data.

One clock rule has a single exception, listed here as the one Pi-clock refusal: a tap on a card the link can't place in
Discord's time (no snowflake id) past its forgetting day by the Pi's clock (the record's `until`, used only for a card with no
snowflake id) is refused as
forgotten: the card gets its Expired line and he is told "This card expired; nothing was done." (`EXPIRED_TAP`). Every other refusal
of a tap is by snowflakes, and a tap or claim with a missing or malformed interaction id is refused (a line on the card,
`TAP_UNSTAMPED`, and a line to Cain; the keeper refuses its request with an "unstamped" notice), never judged at Discord's
time now.
